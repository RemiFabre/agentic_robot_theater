On the turn (Rémi asked what I think): the raise line is the right hinge. It moves the fight from "you cheat" to "you
are a failure", which is what makes him bring up the father on his own, sarcastically, so the clank is motivated and
not a non sequitur. The sigh after "He said it at Thanksgiving" is the beat where the audience expects more self-pity,
and the kids line lands as a bomb instead. Loretta's flip should be all in the voice: line 13 is the only soft line she
has, keep it soft. Line 15 is her only tender line; "Wally looks just like you" is funnier tender than defensive.
